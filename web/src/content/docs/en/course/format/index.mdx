`print` assembles a caption in pieces, and on a long phrase that becomes awkward: the text is
split across lines of the program, and what it will finally look like exists only in your head.

`format` works the other way round: first the whole **template** goes into the buffer, then
values are substituted into it.

<Example scene={TEMPLATE} world={true} buffer={true} />

Press "run". Under the message block you get `copper 120 of 300`: the template
`copper {0} of {1}` went into the buffer first, and two `format`s replaced its placeholders
in turn.

## A placeholder is exactly three characters

A placeholder looks like `{0}`: an opening brace, a digit from 0 to 9, a closing brace.
No spaces, no numbers above nine — such a thing simply does not count as a placeholder, and
`format` will not see it.

<Card kind="error">
If there is no suitable placeholder in the buffer, `format` **does nothing** — silently.
No error, no appending at the end. The value simply disappears, and the caption keeps
a template with an unfilled placeholder.

The most common cause: `format` standing **before** the `print` with the template. The buffer
is empty at that moment, and there is nowhere to substitute into.
</Card>

## The smallest number wins, not the first placeholder

This rule surprises people. `format` searches the buffer for the placeholder with the
**smallest number**, not the one that comes first.

For the template `{1} and {0}` the first `format` substitutes **at the end** — where `{0}` is.
The order of substitutions is set by the numbers, not by their position in the string.

<Card kind="note">
Hence a simple rule: number the placeholders in the order you intend to fill them, not in the
order they stand in the text. Then the phrase can be written the way it reads naturally —
"`{1}` left out of `{0}`" — and still be filled in limit first, remainder second.
</Card>

## A placeholder disappears once filled

A replaced placeholder is gone from the buffer, so the next `format` finds the next one by
number. Two values cannot go into one placeholder, and an extra `format` with none free does
nothing.

| What is in the buffer | `format 120` gives |
| --- | --- |
| `copper {0}` | `copper 120` |
| `{1} and {0}` | `{1} and 120` |
| `copper 0` | `copper 0` — no placeholder, the value is lost |
| empty | empty — nowhere to substitute |

Values turn into text by the same rules as with `print`: a whole number prints without
a fraction, a building as its block's name, emptiness as the word `null`. That is the lesson
<Lesson lesson="print/values">"What prints as what"</Lesson>.

<Card kind="recap">
- `format` substitutes a value into the `{N}` placeholder inside the buffer.
- Template first, substitutions after — otherwise there is nowhere to substitute.
- The placeholder with the smallest number is taken, not the first in the string.
- A placeholder disappears once filled, and an extra `format` silently does nothing.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change the template to `left {1} of {0}` and run it: you get `left 300 of 120` — the numbers
decide what goes where, and the values swapped places even though the `format` lines stayed
the same.

Then remove `{1}` from the template: the second `format` is left without a placeholder and
keeps quiet, and the caption stays `copper 120 of`.
</Card>
