```
ubind @mono
ucontrol mine 9 4 0 0 0
```

The mono reaches the ore with a beam and starts mining: `mining` becomes one, `cargo` grows,
`what` shows copper. In about nine seconds the unit gathers its twenty — no more will fit — and
the mining stops by itself.

| Field | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| first | `mine` |
| second and third | the **tile with ore**, not an item |
| the rest | unused |

The fields hold coordinates, not an item: a unit mines a particular tile, not "copper in
general". <Lesson lesson="ulocate/ore">`ulocate ore`</Lesson> can find such a tile: it gives
the coordinates of the nearest ore of the kind you want.

<Card kind="error">
**A unit does not fly to the ore.** It mines from where it stands, reaching out with a beam:
the tile has to be no further than eight and a half tiles away already at the moment the order
is given. Further, and `mine` simply does nothing — no error and no attempt to fly over.

So a unit is led to distant ore yourself:

```
ucontrol approach 9 4 6 0 0
ucontrol mine 9 4 0 0 0
```

Every miner has the same reach — seventy world units, a little over eight tiles.
</Card>

## Not everyone mines and not everything is minable

Every unit has a mining tier and every ore a hardness. Ore harder than the tier — and the order
silently does nothing:

| Unit | Tier | What it takes | Cargo slots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mono | 1 | sand, scrap, copper, lead | 20 |
| Poly | 2 | and coal as well | 30 |
| Mega | 3 | and titanium as well | 60 |
| Dagger, flare | none | nothing | — |

Ore hardness: sand and scrap are zero, copper and lead one, coal two, titanium three, thorium
four. A unit takes everything no harder than its tier, and thorium on that list goes to nobody:
it needs tier four.

<Card kind="error">
**No refusal and no error.** Ore that is too hard, the wrong tile, the wrong unit — in every
case `mine` simply never starts. You can check with `sensor mining @unit @mining`: a one means
mining is under way.
</Card>

## A full hold ends the work

When the cargo is full, mining stops. The unit stays where it is until somebody unloads it —
it will not fly anywhere by itself.

So a mining program is always a pair of "mine — deliver":

```
ubind @mono
sensor cargo @unit @totalItems
jump 5 greaterThanEq cargo 20
ucontrol mine 9 4 0 0 0
end
ucontrol move 4 4 0 0 0
ucontrol itemDrop container1 20 0 0 0
```

While the hold is not full — mine; once it is — fly to the store and hand over. There is no
need to give a "stop mining" order: a movement command cancels it by itself.

<Card kind="note">
**Mining by units is faster than a drill, not slower.** One unit of ore takes
`50 + hardness * 15` ticks divided by the unit's mining speed: for a mono that is 26 ticks for
copper — nearly two a second. A mechanical drill on four ore tiles gives one every two and
a half seconds.

And there is no lock on a tile: twenty monos can hang over the same ore at once, and each will
mine at full speed. Exactly one drill can stand on that spot.

The price is delivery: a drill puts items on a belt itself, while a unit has to be driven to
the store by a program.
</Card>

<Card kind="recap">
- `ucontrol mine x y` sends a unit to mine a **tile**, not an item.
- A unit mines from where it stands: beyond eight and a half tiles the order does not work.
- What is available to whom is decided by the unit's mining tier and the ore's hardness.
- A full hold stops the mining; unloading is the program's job.
- `sensor @mining` shows whether the work is under way.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change `@mono` in the example to `@flare`: there are no flares on the map, the binding gives
emptiness, and `mining` becomes empty — there is nobody to command. Adding a flare and trying
again will not help either: it cannot mine at all.

Then put the mono back and set the tile to `5 4`. There is no ore there, but the result is not
"nothing": `what` shows `sand`. Sand is dropped by the floor itself rather than by an overlay,
and for mining that is ore like any other — simply the softest.
</Card>
