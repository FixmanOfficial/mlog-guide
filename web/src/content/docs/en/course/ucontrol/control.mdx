<Example scene={OWNER} world={true} />

Two processors are looking at one poly. The upper one commands, the lower one only asks — and
sees somebody else's authority from the outside.

```
sensor who @unit @controller
op equal own who @this
```

`@controller` gives the **block** that is currently driving the unit. In the upper processor
`own` becomes a one; in the lower it stays zero, because `who` there is somebody else's
`processor1`.

## Two properties instead of one

| Property | What it gives |
| --- | --- |
| `@controlled` | a **number**: what kind of driver — a processor, a player, an order |
| `@controller` | an **object**: which block exactly, or the unit itself if logic is not driving it |

`@controlled`'s numbers are not made up; they have names:

| Value | Constant | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0` | — | nobody: the unit lives by its own mind |
| `1` | `@ctrlProcessor` | a processor is driving it |
| `2` | `@ctrlPlayer` | a player is inside it |
| `3` | `@ctrlCommand` | a player has given it an order |

<Card kind="note">
`@controller` on an undriven unit gives **the unit itself**, not emptiness. That is convenient:
the comparison `who == @this` answers "is it mine" the same way whether the unit is free or has
been taken.
</Card>

## Neither of the two answers in full

The properties look like two views of one thing, but each has a blind spot of its own — which
is why a real check takes them together.

<Card kind="error">
**`@controlled` does not tell your processor from somebody else's.** A one means "some
processor is driving it", and that is all. A program that checks only `@controlled` will
happily command a unit a neighbour has already taken — the numbers add up, after all.

Which one exactly is known only to `@controller`, and it has to be compared with `@this`.
</Card>

<Card kind="error">
**`@controller` does not show a player.** It gives a block only when a **processor** is driving
the unit. In every other case — when the unit lives by its own AI and when a player has got
inside it — the answer is the unit itself, one and the same.

A human can only be told apart by `@controlled`: two is a player, three is an order.
</Card>

Hence both lines together, and in this order:

```
sensor ctrl @unit @controlled
jump 0 greaterThan ctrl 1       # taken by a human — leave it alone
sensor who @unit @controller
jump 0 notEqual who @this       # taken by another processor — likewise
```

## Authority is taken away in silence

<Example scene={STOLEN} world={true} />

Here both of them command, and each drags the poly towards its own corner. Watch `own` in both:
the one goes to whichever command ran last, and the unit dangles between two targets.

<Card kind="error">
**Interception does not count as an error.** No refusal, no warning: `ucontrol` simply takes
the unit from its previous owner. A program written on the assumption that "this unit is mine
for ever" starts behaving inexplicably — and it is not its own code that is to blame but the
neighbouring processor.

Hence the rule for working with a squad: **tag your own with a flag and check the tag**, rather
than hoping nobody will touch the unit. That is the <Lesson lesson="ucontrol/flag">lesson about
the flag</Lesson>.
</Card>

## Ten seconds and no more

<Example scene={FORGET} world={true} />

Here the order is given **once**, and after that the program only asks. The poly reaches the
spot — and ten seconds later `ctrl` drops from one to zero, while `who` starts showing the unit
itself. The processor has let it go.

Every order refreshes the term: while `ucontrol` is called in a loop, the authority never runs
out. The moment the calls stop — six hundred ticks, and the unit returns to itself.

<Card kind="note">
It can be let go at once, without waiting out the term: `ucontrol unbind 0 0 0 0 0`. That is
not "forget the unit" — the `@unit` variable stays as it was — but "stop driving it".
</Card>

## Who cannot be taken

Two cases where `ucontrol` keeps quiet however often it is called:

- **a player is driving the unit.** One with somebody inside is not given to logic at all;
- **the unit has an order** — the kind a player hands out by selecting and clicking the map.

In both cases `ubind` works as usual and puts the unit into `@unit` — but the orders never
reach it, and `@controller` will not say so: it goes on giving the unit itself. That leaves
`@controlled`: two and three mean the unit is taken by a human.

<Card kind="recap">
- `@controlled` answers with a number saying what drives a unit; `@controller` with which block.
- On a free unit `@controller` is the unit itself and `@controlled` is zero.
- `@controlled` does not tell your processor from another's, `@controller` does not see
  a player: the full check is both in a row.
- Any `ucontrol` takes a unit from its previous owner, and that is not an error.
- Authority lasts ten seconds from the last order and is extended by every new one.
- Logic will not get a unit under a player or under an order at all.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
In the first example remove the line `ucontrol move 16 6 0 0 0` from the upper processor. There
is nobody left to command: `own` goes to zero in both, `ctrl` becomes zero, and `who` starts
showing the poly itself — it is its own master again.

In the second example remove the line `ucontrol move 18 2 0 0 0` from the lower processor.
The tug of war ends: the poly calmly heads for the upper corner, and `own` in the lower one
stays zero — you may watch as long as you like, watching gives no authority.

In the third example remove the line `jump 3 notEqual ordered 0`. Now the order repeats every
iteration, the term is renewed, and `ctrl` never falls to zero at all.
</Card>
