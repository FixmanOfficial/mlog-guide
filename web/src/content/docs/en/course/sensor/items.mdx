As its third argument `sensor` takes not only a property but also an item. The vault holds 220
copper and 15 graphite; there is no silicon at all, and a vault does not hold water in
principle.

<Example client:load scene={ITEMS} locale="en" />

Three answers to three similar questions:

| Asked | Answer | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `@copper` | 220 | the item is there, and here is how much |
| `@silicon` | 0 | the item exists, but not here — zero means "none" |
| `@water` | `null` | a vault does not hold liquids at all — the question does not apply |

<Card kind="error">
    In an ordinary comparison `null` equals zero, so a check for "no copper left" fires on
    "no block" and "wrong property" too. What to do about that is in the lesson
    [«Emptiness: null»](/en/course/basics/null/).
</Card>

## The first item is an object

`@firstItem` gives not a count but the item itself: the same object as the constant `@copper`.
It is convenient to pass on — into `ucontrol itemTake`, into `sensor` as the next argument,
into `print`.

"First" means the one that has been in the block longest and has not run out, not the largest
and not the first alphabetically. On a drill it is whatever it is mining.

## What can be asked as an item

Any item and any liquid in the game: `@copper`, `@graphite`, `@water`, `@cryofluid`. The full
list is in the [reference](/en/reference/instructions/lookup/), along with a way to walk them
all by index.

<Card kind="note">
**The course sandbox has no liquids**: the blocks that hold them are always empty. The numbers
about items are real, the ones about liquids are not.
</Card>

<Card kind="recap">
- As its third field `sensor` takes not only a property but also an item or a liquid.
- "There is none of the item" is zero; "the question does not apply" is `null`.
- `@firstItem` gives the item itself as an object, not its count.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Click the pencil by the property in any line and ask the vault for three more things:
`@graphite` (it is there), `@titanium` (not in this vault, but it exists as an item) and
`@sand`. The first answers with a number, the others with zero.

Then type `@vault` into the block's field instead of `vault1` — ask the same thing of the
vault's type rather than of the vault. The answer is not about contents but about **price**:
`sensor @vault @titanium` gives 250, the amount of titanium a vault costs. A blueprint has no
contents but it does have a cost, and this is the only way to learn it from logic.

In infinite-resources mode the price honestly answers zero — just like building, which deducts
nothing.
</Card>
