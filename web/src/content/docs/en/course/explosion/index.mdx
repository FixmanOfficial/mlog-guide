| Field | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| first | **whose explosion it is**: it will not touch its own |
| second and third | the point in tiles |
| fourth | the radius in tiles |
| fifth | the damage |
| sixth and seventh | whether to hit air and ground |
| eighth | whether to pierce: hit everybody through, or only the first |
| ninth | whether to show a flash |

## The team decides who gets hurt

The first field is not "who to hit" but **whose explosion this is**. Everybody not on that team
suffers.

```
explosion @sharded 10 5 3 200 1 1 0 0   # your explosion, hits enemies
explosion @crux 10 5 3 200 1 1 0 0      # the enemy's, hits you
```

It is easy to mix up, and the mistake is a quiet one: the program dutifully explodes and the
target stands there untouched — the explosion simply turned out to be "its own".

<Card kind="note">
The sixth and seventh fields separate air from ground. An explosion told to hit the ground only
will not touch a flare or a poly — that is how ground mines are made in scripts.
</Card>

<Card kind="error">
**Damage falls off towards the edge of the radius.** At the point of the explosion it is full,
and at the boundary about 40% remains — only with piercing on (the eighth field) does the
circle cover everybody equally.

So "set a bigger radius" is not the same as "hit harder": a distant enemy gets its share and
survives.
</Card>

## What it is used for

An explosion is a scripting tool, needed where damage has to happen **for no reason**:

- traps and mines on a map: the player stepped into a forbidden zone — explosion;
- events: a meteorite, a collapse, sabotage;
- punishment for breaking a script's rule — for building in a forbidden area, for instance;
- finishing off: the script wants the wave to end right now.

```
query circle unit @crux 10 5 3 0
sensor amount @queries @size
jump 0 equal amount 0
explosion @sharded 10 5 3 200 1 1 0 0
```

<Card kind="error">
**An explosion hits buildings too.** A three-tile radius in the middle of a base will not just
remove an enemy: everything not on your team takes damage — derelict structures and walls
included.

The program has to check who is in the radius itself: <Lesson lesson="query">`query`</Lesson>
over the same area and the same team.
</Card>

<Card kind="recap">
- `explosion team x y radius damage air ground pierce flash` deals area damage.
- The first field is whose explosion it is; it does not touch its own.
- The radius and coordinates are in tiles, the damage an ordinary number.
- Air and ground are separated from each other.
- Buildings in the radius take damage too.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change the team in the example from `@sharded` to `@crux`: the explosion becomes the enemy's
and the daggers survive it — `enemies` stays at two.

Then put `@sharded` back and set the radius to `10`: the second dagger is covered too, but it
survives the hit — it is six tiles away, and less than a quarter of two hundred damage reaches
it.
</Card>
