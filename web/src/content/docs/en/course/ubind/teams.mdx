There are two daggers on the map, both hostile. `ubind @dagger` honestly searches — and puts
emptiness into `@unit`: the walk goes **through units of your own team**, and there are no
enemies on that list at all.

The same goes for binding by object: even if an enemy unit is sitting in one of your
variables — found by a radar, say — `ubind` will not take it.

<Card kind="error">
**No unit and an enemy unit look identical.** In both cases `@unit` is empty, and the binding
will not tell them apart. If a program expects your polys and gets emptiness, either there are
none or they are not yours.
</Card>

## What you can still do with enemies

The ban covers driving only. Nobody stops you looking at an enemy:

| What | With what |
| --- | --- |
| find the nearest | <Lesson lesson="radar/find">`radar`</Lesson> from a building, `uradar` from your own unit |
| walk through all of them | <Lesson lesson="fetch">`fetch`</Lesson> by team, <Lesson lesson="query">`query`</Lesson> by area — both on a world processor |
| read properties | `sensor` — health, coordinates, type, team |
| shoot at it | <Lesson lesson="control/shoot">`control shootp`</Lesson> on a turret |
| aim your own unit at it | `ucontrol targetp` — the target is given to yours, not to theirs |

```
radar enemy any any distance duo1 1 foe
sensor health foe @health
sensor team foe @team
```

An enemy unit here is an ordinary value: it is read, compared, passed around. Only one thing is
forbidden — putting it into `@unit` and commanding it.

## A team arrives as a number

`sensor whose unit @team` gives the team's **number**, not the team itself: derelict is zero,
yours is usually one, the enemy's is two.

The constants `@sharded`, `@crux`, `@derelict`, on the other hand, are objects, and they do not
compare with a number directly. The number is asked of the team itself:

```
sensor whose unit @team
sensor crux @crux @id
jump 10 equal whose crux
```

A processor learns its own team the same way, through itself: `sensor myTeam @this @team`.

<Card kind="note">
**A team is no obstacle to a world processor.** It binds to an enemy unit if that unit is
sitting in a variable, and drives it like its own — half the campaign's scripts rest on this.

But even for it the walk by type goes through its own team: to reach an enemy, the unit must
first be **found as an object** — by radar, by <Lesson lesson="query">`query`</Lesson> over an
area or by <Lesson lesson="fetch">`fetch`</Lesson> by index in the enemy team — and only then
bound.
</Card>

<Card kind="recap">
- `ubind` takes only units of your own team — by type and by object alike.
- An enemy unit and no unit are indistinguishable: emptiness in both cases.
- An enemy can be read with anything: `radar`, `uradar`, `sensor`.
- `sensor @team` gives a team's number; a constant's number comes from `sensor @crux @id`.
- A world processor binds to an enemy unit by object.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change `ubind @dagger` in the example to `ubind @poly`: the poly on the map is yours, so the
binding succeeds — `theirs` stops being empty, even though the variable's name has not changed.

Then add `sensor myTeam @this @team` at the end and compare it with `team`: for your own unit the
number is the same as the processor's — one.
</Card>
