By default a processor works without stopping: it runs its instructions for the tick and
carries on from the same place on the next one. A three-line program gets through an iteration
several hundred times a second — and almost always that is wasted.

`wait` says "not right now". The processor gives up the rest of the tick and comes back to the
same line later, when the time is up.

Its neighbours in the category do something similar in their own ways:
[`end`](/en/course/end/) starts the iteration over, [`stop`](/en/course/stop/) halts the
processor altogether.
