`select` puts one of two values into a variable: the first if the condition holds, the second
if it does not. In the block it reads as words: `total = if copper >= 100 then 1 else 0`.

Before picking it up it is worth understanding when it is *not* needed — otherwise people start
writing with it what is shorter without it.

<Example client:load scene={CHOICE} world={true} locale="en" />

Press "step" four times.

## When `select` is not needed

The example's second line is `op greaterThanEq enough copper 100`, and `enough` ends up
holding a one. No choice was required: **a comparison answers with a one or a zero by itself**,
and that is already a finished value.

<Card kind="error">
So `select enough >= copper 100 1 0` is wasted work: the same one and zero come out of a single
`op`. If both of your values are 1 and 0, what you need is not `select` but a comparison.
More on that in the lesson [«Comparisons and logic»](/en/course/op/logic/).
</Card>

## When it is the right tool

As soon as the values stop being one and zero, a comparison stops helping.

**The third line** chooses between two strings: `caption` becomes "enough". That cannot be had
through `op` at all — a comparison can only answer with a number.

**The fourth line** — `select howMany lessThan copper 100 copper 100` — caps a number from
above: if there is less than a hundred copper, take what there is; otherwise take exactly
a hundred. `howMany` ends up as 100.

The general rule: `select` is needed when **both values are arbitrary** — numbers other than
zero and one, strings, items, buildings, variables.

| What is being chosen | With what |
| --- | --- |
| a one or a zero by a condition | `op` with a comparison |
| one of two ready values | `select` |
| one of two actions | `jump`: that is a branch, not a value |
| the larger or smaller of two numbers | `op max` and `op min` — shorter |

## It chooses a value, not an action

Both halves of `select` are **values** that already exist: numbers, strings, variables,
objects. The instruction runs nothing and skips nothing, it simply takes one of two.

<Card kind="error">
Calculations cannot be hidden in the fields: `total = if … then a+1 else a-1` cannot be
written, because `a+1` is an operation, not a value. You compute in advance, in two `op` lines,
and `select` takes what is already computed.

And both halves are computed — including the one that will not be taken. If the calculation is
expensive, `jump` is cheaper: it does skip what is not needed.
</Card>

## The conditions are the same as `jump`'s

There are eight of them, and the buttons carry the same signs — `==`, `===`, `not` and the rest
from [the table](/en/course/jump/branch/). The words "if", "then" and "else" in the block are
a translation; without it they are `if`, `then`, `else`.

<Card kind="recap">
- `select` puts one of two ready values into a variable by a condition.
- If the values are one and zero, `select` is not needed: the comparison itself gives the answer.
- Only it can choose between strings, items and arbitrary numbers.
- An action is chosen by branching, not by `select`.
- The conditions and their labels are the same as `jump`'s.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change the condition in the third line to `lessThan` and run the example again: `caption`
becomes "low", although there is still 120 copper. The values stayed where they were — the
first is still the one for "true", it is just that "true" now means something else.

Then type `copper` instead of `100` in the second half of the fourth line: the cap disappears,
and `howMany` equals the copper whatever the condition. That shows both halves are ordinary
values, and nothing stops you putting the same thing in both.
</Card>
