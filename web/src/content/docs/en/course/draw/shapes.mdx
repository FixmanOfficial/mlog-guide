Everything the display can do is drawn here: a fill and an outline, a line, a hexagon,
a triangle in outline and a triangle filled.

## What each shape asks for

| Command | Fields | What it counts from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rect` | `x y width height` | the bottom left corner |
| `lineRect` | `x y width height` | the same, but in outline |
| `line` | `x1 y1 x2 y2` | point to point |
| `poly` | `x y sides radius rotation` | **from the centre** |
| `linePoly` | `x y sides radius rotation` | the same, in outline |
| `triangle` | `x1 y1 x2 y2 x3 y3` | three vertices |

That difference in what a shape counts from is the most common cause of "it drew in the wrong
place". For a rectangle it is a corner, for a polygon the centre.

<Card kind="note">
There is no circle among the commands: you draw one as a polygon with many sides. No more than
**25** of them, though — anything above that the display silently trims to twenty-five. That is
the limit of smoothness: on 176 dots, twenty-five sides are indistinguishable from a circle.
</Card>

## Line thickness

```
draw stroke 4
```

`stroke` sets the thickness of everything drawn in outline: `line`, `lineRect`, `linePoly`.
Like the colour, it holds until the next `stroke`, and it does not affect fills at all.

<Card kind="note">
Thickness is counted in the same picture dots as the coordinates — not in screen pixels.
An ordinary display has 80 per side, a large one 176, so the same numbers on a large display
give a smaller-looking shape: it takes up a smaller share of the screen.
</Card>

## Rotating a polygon

The fifth field of `poly` is an angle in degrees, and it is counted as everywhere in mlog:
counter-clockwise, with zero pointing right.

```
draw poly 88 88 3 40 0
draw poly 88 88 3 40 180
```

Two triangles from the same point, rotated relative to each other — a star. Half the pictures
on displays are built on that.

<Card kind="recap">
- `rect` and `lineRect` count from a corner, `poly` and `linePoly` from the centre.
- A circle is drawn as a polygon; there can be at most 25 sides, the extra are trimmed silently.
- `stroke` sets outline thickness and holds, like the colour.
- Rotation is given in degrees, counter-clockwise.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change `draw poly 45 40 6 30 0` in the example to `draw poly 45 40 25 30 0` and run it: the
hexagon becomes a circle. Put 40 and the picture does not change at all — a display does not
draw more than 25 sides.

Then set `draw stroke 1` and look at the line: it becomes a hair. And with `draw stroke 20`
the line covers a third of the display — thickness grows both ways from its middle.
</Card>
