There are three on the map: a dagger and a flare belong to the enemy, a poly is ours. Three
radars find different ones — through filters.

## The instruction's fields

| Field | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| first, second, third | the **filters**: three conditions joined by a logical AND |
| fourth | what measures "best" — that is <Lesson lesson="radar/sort">the next lesson</Lesson> |
| fifth | **where we look from**: the block being asked |
| sixth | the sort direction |
| seventh | where to put the one found |

## Eight filters

| Filter | Who qualifies |
| --- | --- |
| `any` | anybody |
| `enemy` | a unit of another team |
| `ally` | a unit of your own team |
| `flying` | airborne |
| `ground` | on the ground |
| `attacker` | able to shoot |
| `player` | driven by a player |
| `boss` | a wave boss |

The derelict team does not count as an enemy: rusty units on the map will not come up under
`enemy`.

Three filters are an **AND**, not an OR: `enemy flying any` finds one that is hostile **and**
airborne. To have fewer than three conditions, the spare ones are set to `any`.

<Card kind="error">
**A radar does not see buildings.** Neither yours nor anyone else's: it walks units only. To
find a block, a processor has its links (<Lesson lesson="getlink">`getlink`</Lesson>), and the
map has the world instructions.
</Card>

## Where it looks from

The fifth field is a block, and the range is taken from it:

| Block | How far it sees |
| --- | --- |
| a turret | as far as it shoots |
| a processor | as far as it links |
| every other block | not at all |

So a radar is usually hung on the very turret that will be commanded afterwards: it sees
exactly what it can reach.

<Card kind="note">
**The course sandbox has neither players nor waves**, so the `player` and `boss` filters always
give emptiness here. In the game they work as usual.
</Card>

<Card kind="recap">
- A radar looks for **one** unit: the best by the measure in the fourth field.
- Three filters are joined by a logical AND; the unneeded ones are set to `any`.
- The range is taken from the block that is looking.
- A radar does not find buildings at all.
</Card>

<Card kind="task">
Change the first radar in the example from `enemy any any` to `enemy ground any` — `near`
gets the dagger and the flare drops out: it flies.

Then give the third radar `ally ground any`: the answer becomes `null`. The poly is airborne,
there are no other allies on the map, and that is not an error but an honest "nobody".
</Card>
